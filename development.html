Feedback from Peers and Tutors

Peer Feedback: Collaborative Discussion 1

Three peers responded to my initial post on the Southwest Airlines scheduling collapse:

  • Emily argued that regulatory penalties are “necessary but insufficient”, since a US$140 million fine can be absorbed as a cost of doing business, and proposed cloud-based failover scheduling, modernisation driven by staff feedback and stress testing for extreme weather.
  • Sarah noted that other airlines recovered quickly, showing resilience was possible, and suggested that benchmarking and sharing best practice build resilience faster than penalties.
  • Essa agreed that legacy-system risk is a strategic rather than an IT issue, and recommended earlier upgrades, disruption simulations and stronger contingency procedures.

All three supported my argument that penalties alone would not drive modernisation, and added practical measures I had not covered.

Tutor Feedback: Team Project

The tutor praised our clear business question, methodology and model comparison, but found the segment discussion “relatively descriptive” and asked for deeper critical discussion of model limitations. The full feedback is on the Team Project page. I acted on it in the Unit 6 seminar tasks by testing whether clusters were stable and meaningful.
