Feedback from Peers and Tutors

Peer Feedback: Collaborative Discussion 1

Three peers responded to my initial post on the Southwest Airlines scheduling collapse:

  • Emily argued that regulatory penalties are “necessary but insufficient”, since a US$140 million fine can be absorbed as a cost of doing business, and proposed cloud-based failover scheduling, modernisation driven by staff feedback and stress testing for extreme weather.
  • Sarah noted that other airlines recovered quickly, showing resilience was possible, and suggested that benchmarking and sharing best practice build resilience faster than penalties.
  • Essa agreed that legacy-system risk is a strategic rather than an IT issue, and recommended earlier upgrades, disruption simulations and stronger contingency procedures.

All three supported my argument that penalties alone would not drive modernisation, and added practical measures I had not covered.

Tutor Feedback: Team Project

The tutor praised our clear business question, methodology and model comparison, but found the segment discussion “relatively descriptive” and asked for deeper critical discussion of model limitations. The full feedback is on the Team Project page. I acted on it in the Unit 6 seminar tasks by testing whether clusters were stable and meaningful.

Professional Development Plan (PDP)

My goal is to apply machine learning in business and finance. The plan targets the gaps above, with the weakest areas first.

Commercial awareness: turning model results into business decisions

Actions: Open every analysis with the business question and the decision it supports; quantify the cost of each error type; translate metrics into business terms (for example, % of cases within an acceptable error).

Resources and support: Unit 6 tutor feedback as a checklist; colleagues in finance as reviewers.

How I will measure progress: Next assignment feedback no longer cites weak business interpretation; one work analysis reviewed by a business stakeholder.

Target: March 2027

Teamwork and leadership: making contributions visible

Actions: At the first meeting, agree roles, deadlines and a shared contribution log; keep short meeting notes with decisions and actions; list all members and their contributions in team deliverables.

Resources and support: Tuckman (1965) and the social-loafing literature (Latané, Williams and Harkins, 1979); module team tools.

How I will measure progress: Complete meeting notes and contribution log for the next team module; workload shared more evenly.

Target: Next team module

Interpersonal: raising issues early

Actions: Raise uneven participation with the team in the first week it appears, and with the tutor if it continues; give specific, constructive peer feedback.

Resources and support: University study skills resources; tutor.

How I will measure progress: Issues raised and recorded within one week; no last-minute workload crisis.

Target: Ongoing from next module

Technical: explainable and validated models for tabular financial data

Actions: Build a credit-risk or customer-churn project with SHAP explanations, time-ordered validation and per-segment error analysis; publish it with a model card and a datasheet.

Resources and support: Mitchell et al. (2019); Gebru et al. (2021); scikit-learn and SHAP documentation; public financial datasets.

How I will measure progress: Project published on GitHub with model card, datasheet and segment-level results.

Target: February 2027

Ethical and regulatory awareness in finance

Actions: Study the EU AI Act's obligations for high-risk systems, including creditworthiness assessment, and map them to each step of my ML workflow.

Resources and support: European Union (2024).

How I will measure progress: A one-page checklist that I apply to my next financial model.

Target: January 2027

IT and digital: deployment and monitoring

Actions: Deploy one model as an API with logging of inputs, confidence and drift, and a documented retraining trigger.

Resources and support: Cloud ML platform free tier; the Unit 11 deployment design.

How I will measure progress: A working endpoint with a monitoring dashboard.

Target: May 2027

Time management and critical reflection

Actions: Plan assignments backwards from the deadline with a one-week buffer; keep a short monthly reflective log (What / So what / Now what).

Resources and support: Rolfe, Freshwater and Jasper (2001).

How I will measure progress: Drafts finished one week before deadlines; twelve log entries over the year.

Target: Monthly, reviewed October 2027
